# Signs of AI Writing — Reference Catalog

Distilled from Wikipedia's field guide **"Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing"** (shortcut **WP:AISIGNS**), the same page featured in the @yatesvids "Signs of AI writing" reel. This catalog exists to be *avoided*: it is the inverse of the field guide. Where the guide helps editors *detect* undisclosed AI text, this reference helps writers *not produce it*.

> Attribution: patterns, examples, and era breakdowns below are drawn from [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), a WikiProject AI Cleanup advice page (CC BY-SA 4.0). Examples are lightly trimmed for brevity.

---

## 1. The "AI vocabulary" list

LLMs measurably overuse these words (research on ChatGPT output, academic abstracts, and Wikipedia text post-2022). They co-occur: where there is one, there are likely others.

### All-era banned list

additionally (sentence-initial), align with, boasts (="has"), bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, key (adjective), landscape (abstract), meticulous/meticulously, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant

### By LLM era

| Era | Words |
|---|---|
| 2023 – mid-2024 (GPT-4) | additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate/intricacies, interplay, key, landscape, meticulous, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant |
| mid-2024 – mid-2025 (GPT-4o) | align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant |
| mid-2025+ (GPT-5) | emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing + notability phrasing (section 5.2) |
| Grok | causal, empirical, correlate, underscore |

**Real AI example** (from the guide, December 2024 revision of *Jameh Mosque of Ashtarjan*):

> The inscriptions also offer **valuable** insights into the construction of the mosque. They record the names of the **key** craftsmen involved… These names **highlight** the collaborative nature of mosque construction and **emphasize** the contributions of skilled artisans… also **underscores** the **enduring** practice of pious patronage…

Five tells in three sentences. The human version: *"The inscriptions record the names of the craftsmen involved, including Mason Ahmad b. Muhammad. They show the collaborative nature of mosque construction and the role of skilled artisans…"*

**Real AI example** (May 2025 revision of *Somali people*):

> Somali cuisine is an **intricate** and diverse fusion… drawing from the rich **tapestry** of Arab, Indian, and Italian flavours. This culinary **tapestry** is a direct result of Somalia's longstanding heritage of **vibrant** trade…

## 2. Copulative avoidance ("is"/"are" avoidance)

LLMs replace simple "is/are/has" with fancier verbs. Studies measured >10% decline of "is"/"are" in academic writing after 2023, and the same pattern in AI copyedits of Wikipedia.

| AI pattern | Human pattern |
|---|---|
| serves as / stands as / marks / functions as / operates as / represents [a] | is / are |
| boasts / features / maintains / offers [a] | has |
| "X refers to…" (lead sentence) | "X is…" |
| "ventured into politics as a candidate" | "was a candidate" |
| "began his career as…" | "was…" |

**Real rewrite pair** (from the guide):

- ✗ AI: "Gallery 825 on La Cienega Boulevard **serves as** LAAA's exhibition space for contemporary art. The gallery **features** four separate spaces…"
- ✓ Human: "Gallery 825 on La Cienega Boulevard, purchased in 1958, **is** LAAA's exhibition arm for contemporary art. **There are** four individual gallery spaces…"

## 3. Negative parallelisms

LLMs lean on rhetorical "not/but" constructions, often repeatedly:

- "Not only X, but also Y" / "It is not just X, it's Y"
- "Not X, but Y" / "no X, no Y, just Z"
- "X rather than Y"

**Real example** (April 2025 revision of *Self-portrait (Yayoi Kusama)* — three stacked in one paragraph):

> This dispersal **is not dissolution. Rather, it constitutes** what Deleuze might describe as "becoming"… Kusama's self-portrait **is not a mirror but a portal**: **not a representation of self, but a mechanism** for its constant reinvention.

## 4. Rule of three

LLMs overuse triads to make superficial analyses look comprehensive: "adjective, adjective, adjective", "short phrase, short phrase, and short phrase".

**Real example** (*Rotary saw*, July 2024): "**Standard Rotary Saws**… **Heavy-Duty Rotary Saws**… **Corded and Cordless Versions**…" — then, for "Construction and Renovation: For cutting **drywall, plywood, and other construction materials**. Electrical and Plumbing: To create openings for **electrical outlets, switches, and plumbing fixtures**. Hobby and Craft: Used in **model making, woodworking, and other craft projects**."

## 5. Content-level tells

### 5.1 Undue emphasis on significance, legacy, and broader trends

Words to watch: *stands/serves as*, *is a testament/reminder*, *a crucial/pivotal/vital/significant/key role or moment*, *underscores/highlights its importance*, *reflects broader*, *symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting*, *contributing to the*, *setting the stage for*, *marking/shaping the*, *represents/marks a shift*, *key turning point*, *evolving landscape*, *focal point*, *indelible mark*, *deeply rooted*.

**Real examples**:

> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, **marking a pivotal moment** in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain. […] This initiative **was part of a broader movement** across Spain to decentralize administrative functions.

> The phenomenon has **generated debate** about authenticity, consent, and the psychological effects of digitally extending personhood. […] GriefBots have **prompted broader reflection** on mortality and memory in a digital age. They blur boundaries between life and data, **raising philosophical questions** about identity…

LLMs even do this for mundane topics (etymology, population data), and about species: "It **plays a role in the ecosystem** and **contributes to Hawaii's rich cultural heritage**… **Preserving this endemic species is vital** not only for ecological diversity but also…" — even when no conservation status exists.

### 5.2 Canned emphasis on notability, attribution, and media coverage

Words to watch: *independent coverage*, *local/regional/national/[country] media outlets*, *music/business/tech outlets*, *trade publications*, *profiled in*, *written by a leading expert*, *active social media presence*, *featured in … and other prominent media outlets*.

**Real example**:

> She spoke about AI on CNN, and was **featured in** Vogue, Wired, Toronto Star, and **other media**. […] Her insights have also been **featured in** Wired, Refinery29, and **other prominent media outlets**.

> The mall **maintains a strong digital presence**, particularly on Instagram…

### 5.3 Superficial analyses (tacked-on "-ing" phrases)

Words to watch: *highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing…*, *ensuring…*, *reflecting/symbolizing…*, *contributing to…*, *cultivating/fostering…*, *encompassing…*, *enhancing…*, *valuable insights*, *align/resonate with*.

**Real examples**:

> As of the April 2008 census, the population of Douera stood at approximately 56,998 inhabitants, **creating a lively community within its borders**. Situated in the central-north region of the country, Douera enjoys close proximity to the capital, Algiers, **further enhancing its significance as a dynamic hub of activity and culture**.

> Historically, it has been crucial for linking Darbhanga with significant cities like Delhi, Patna, and Kolkata, **facilitating the movement of passengers and goods**. […] Over the years, Darbhanga Junction has seen several upgrades and modernization efforts… **reflecting its continued relevance in the regional and national transportation landscape**.

### 5.4 Promotional and advertisement-like language

Words to watch: *boasts a*, *vibrant*, *rich*, *profound*, *enhancing*, *showcasing*, *exemplifies*, *commitment to*, *natural beauty*, *nestled*, *in the heart of*, *groundbreaking*, *renowned*, *featuring*, *diverse array*.

**Real example**:

> **Nestled** within the **breathtaking** region of Gonder in Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo **stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage**… From its scenic landscapes to its historical landmarks, Alamata Raya Kobo **offers visitors a fascinating glimpse into the diverse tapestry** of Ethiopia. In this article, we will explore the **unique characteristics** that make Alamata Raya Kobo **a town worth visiting**…

(Also a §6.1 meta-commentary example: "In this article, we will explore…")

### 5.5 Vague attributions and overgeneralization

Words to watch: *industry reports*, *observers have cited*, *experts argue*, *some critics argue*, *several sources/publications* (when few are cited), *researchers and conservationists*, *described in scholarship*, *modern researchers treat*.

**Real example**:

> Due to its unique characteristics, the Haolai River is of interest to **researchers and conservationists**. Efforts are ongoing to monitor its ecological health… (no sources named)

### 5.6 Outline-like "Challenges and Future Prospects" sections

The formula: "Despite its [positive words], [subject] faces challenges…" → list → "Despite these challenges, [subject] continues to thrive / remains vital…" → vague speculation about the future. Section headings like "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook", "Challenges and Future Directions".

**Real examples**:

> **Despite its industrial and residential prosperity, Korattur faces challenges** typical of urban areas… With its **strategic location and ongoing initiatives**, Korattur **continues to thrive** as an integral part of the Ambattur industrial zone…

> **Despite their promising applications, pyroelectric materials face several challenges**… **Despite these challenges**, the versatility of pyroelectric materials **positions them as critical components** for sustainable energy solutions…

## 6. Communication tells (chatbot residue)

### 6.1 Meta-commentary and collaborative chat phrasing

Words to watch: *I hope this helps*, *Of course!*, *Certainly!*, *You're absolutely right!*, *Would you like…*, *is there anything else*, *let me know*, *more detailed breakdown*, *here is a*.

**Real examples**:

> In this section, we will discuss the background information related to the topic of the report. This will include a discussion of relevant literature… (*Metaphysics*, Aug 2023)

> If you plan to add this information to the "Animal Cruelty Controversy" section of Foshan's Wikipedia page, ensure that the content is presented in a neutral tone… (*Foshan*, Mar 2025 — instructions to the editor left in the article)

### 6.2 Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and didactic notes

Words to watch: *it's important/critical/crucial to note/remember/consider*, *worth noting*, *may vary*, *as of my last knowledge cutoff*, *I don't have access to real-time information*.

**Real example**:

> However, **it's important to note** that these caucuses operate outside the formal ANC structure and their influence on policy decisions **may vary**.

### 6.3 Phrasal templates and placeholder text

LLMs emit fill-in-the-blank templates; users forget to fill them.

**Real examples**:

> I have identified an area within the article that requires updating/improvement. **[Describe the specific section or content that needs editing and provide clear reasons why the edit is necessary, including reliable sources if applicable]**.

> We hope to resubmit our work once these changes have been made. Thank you for your understanding… Best regards, **[Your Name]** and Chloe

## 7. Markup and formatting tells

| Tell | Detail |
|---|---|
| Markdown in non-Markdown contexts | `**bold**`, `## headings`, `[text](url)`, ``` fences, `#` lists inside wikitext/docs |
| Title-case headings | "Impact of Technology and Digitalization" |
| Overuse of boldface | "key takeaways" style; bolding every occurrence of a term |
| Inline-header vertical lists | "**Route Details:** …" bullet lists; explicit "1." numbers instead of real list syntax |
| Emoji as formatting | emoji before headings/bullets; emoji in formal prose |
| Em dash overuse | formulaic, usually *spaced* ("word — word") |
| Curly quotes | curly “ ” and ’ (ChatGPT/DeepSeek habit), especially inconsistent mixes |
| Skipping heading levels | jumping H2 → H4 |
| Thematic breaks before headings | `---` used decoratively |
| Unusual tables | tables where prose would do |

## 8. Signs of human writing (emulate these)

From the guide's own section on human indicators — the inverse checklist:

- **Simple is/has phrases**: "there is a…", "it has a…".
- **Plain verbs over stiff synonyms**: wrote (not authored), moved (not relocated), used (not utilized), tried (not attempted), died (not passed away).
- **Superlative or definitive statements**: "one of the best", "is the only", "was the first".
- **Hedging qualifiers and intensifiers**: very, perhaps, tends to.
- **Wordy constructions**: "as a result of", "in order to", "all of the", "a part of", "the fact that".
- **Natural word repetition** (no elegant variation).
- **Uneven sentence rhythm**; paragraphs that don't each end in a summary.

## 9. Ineffective indicators — do not "overcorrect" into these

The guide warns that these do *not* prove AI, and conversely they are not what "human" means. Avoid caricaturing humanness:

- Perfect grammar alone — many humans write perfectly.
- Mixed casual/formal registers — a human trait, not proof of AI.
- "Bland" or "robotic" prose — LLM output skews *positive and verbose*, not robotic.
- Fancy/academic prose — only *specific words* are overused, not formality in general.
- Transition words in isolation — "Additionally," "Consequently," "Notably" at sentence start are a weak tell; a single use is fine.
- Unsourced content — not an AI marker.
- Intentional errors, typos, or "imperfections" — do **not** add these to look human. Competent humans write correctly; errors are a tell of faking, and this skill is about natural style, not impersonation.

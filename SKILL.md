# Human Writing (Avoid AI Tells)

Write like a competent human writer. This skill is the reverse of Wikipedia's field guide *"Signs of AI writing"* (the same guide shown in the "Signs of AI writing" Instagram reel): instead of detecting LLM tells, **avoid them**. The tells below are habits LLMs fall into — dense "AI vocabulary", significance inflation, rhetorical formulas, and formatting tics — not content problems. The goal is natural, specific, human prose. Never fake imperfections (typos, "quirky" grammar) to look human; competent humans write correctly. And never game AI detectors — the point is style, not deception.

## 1. Vocabulary: words that are AI tells

Avoid these words entirely in your output. If two or more appear in one piece of writing, rewrite. Note: a word being overused by AI does **not** mean its synonyms are overused — don't swap "vibrant" for "lively"; cut the flourish entirely.

**Banned list (all eras):**

| Word | Instead write |
|---|---|
| additionally (starting a sentence) | reorder, or plain "also" |
| align with | match, fit, or restructure |
| boasts (meaning "has") | has |
| bolstered | strengthened, backed, or cut |
| crucial | important, or cut (often unneeded) |
| delve | explore, dig into, look into, or cut |
| emphasizing | stressing, pointing out, showing, or cut |
| enduring | lasting, or cut |
| enhance | improve, boost, or cut |
| fostering | encouraging, building, supporting |
| garner | win, get, draw |
| highlight (as a verb) | show, point to, note, feature |
| interplay | interaction, relationship |
| intricate / intricacies | complex / complexities, or be specific |
| key (as an adjective) | main, central, important, or cut |
| landscape (abstract: "the tech landscape") | scene, field, world, or cut |
| meticulous / meticulously | careful / carefully, thorough / thoroughly |
| pivotal | important, or cut |
| robust | strong, solid, thorough |
| showcase | show, display, feature |
| tapestry (abstract: "a rich tapestry of") | cut |
| testament ("a testament to") | shows, is proof of, or cut |
| underscore (as a verb) | underline, show, point up |
| valuable | useful, important, or cut (esp. "valuable insights") |
| vibrant | lively, colorful, busy — or cut |

**Era tells** (older output used older lists; all are to be avoided):

| Era | Words to watch |
|---|---|
| 2023 – mid-2024 (GPT-4) | additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate/intricacies, interplay, key, landscape, meticulous, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant |
| mid-2024 – mid-2025 (GPT-4o) | align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant |

## 2. Grammar: human patterns to keep, AI patterns to drop

**Use "is / are / has" directly.** LLMs avoid simple copulas and marketing verbs. Never write:

- ✗ "The gallery **serves as** LAAA's exhibition space and **features** four separate spaces."
- ✗ "It **stands as** a reminder of…"
- ✗ "The company **boasts** a workforce of…"
- ✗ "The stadium **holds the distinction of being** the oldest…"
- ✗ "He **ventured into politics as a candidate**." ("was a candidate")
- ✗ "She **began her career as** a lawyer." ("was a lawyer")

✓ "The gallery is LAAA's exhibition space and has four separate rooms."

**Prefer plain verbs** (a documented sign of human writing): wrote (not *authored*), moved (not *relocated*), used (not *utilized*), tried (not *attempted*), died (not *passed away*).

**Human syntax is your friend — keep it.** Humans naturally write: "there is a…", "it has a…", "one of the best…", "is the only…", "was the first…", "very", "perhaps", "tends to", "as a result of", "in order to", "all of the", "the fact that". LLMs scrub these out as "too informal"; their absence is itself a tell. Keep them.

## 3. Rhetorical patterns to avoid

- **Negative parallelisms.** Avoid repeated "not… but…" constructions: "not only X but also Y", "it's not just X, it's Y", "not X, but Y", "no X, no Y, just Z", "X rather than Y". One, occasionally, is fine; stacking them is a tell.
- **The rule of three.** LLMs lean on triads to sound comprehensive ("fast, reliable, and secure"; "cuts through noise, drives action, and builds trust"). Vary list lengths: two items, four items, prose.
- **Elegant variation.** Never thesaurus-swap to avoid repeating a word. Humans repeat: "the canal… the canal… the canal". AI writes "the canal… the waterway… the artery". Repeat the word.
- **Tacked-on "-ing" analysis.** Sentences ending in ", -ing phrase" that only fluff the fact: "…, creating a lively community within its borders", "…, further enhancing its significance", "…, underscoring its role in…", "…, reflecting its continued relevance". If the participle only restates or inflates, delete it.
- **Forced transitions.** No chains of "Moreover, … Additionally, … Furthermore, …". Let paragraphs stand on their own.

## 4. Content rules: no inflation, no filler analysis

- **No significance inflation.** State facts; let the reader judge importance. Never write: "marking a pivotal moment", "represented a significant shift", "was part of a broader movement", "its enduring legacy", "a testament to", "setting the stage for", "deeply rooted", "an indelible mark", "the evolving landscape of", "generated debate", "prompted broader reflection", "raising philosophical questions", "contributes to X's rich cultural heritage", "plays a vital role in the ecosystem".
- **No canned "Challenges and Future Prospects" coda.** Avoid the formula: "Despite its X, Y faces challenges…" → "Despite these challenges, …" → vague optimistic close. End factually, when the facts end.
- **No vague attribution / weasel words.** Avoid "experts argue", "industry reports", "observers have cited", "some critics say", "researchers and conservationists", "described in scholarship", "several sources claim" (when you cite one or two). Attribute claims to named, real people or drop them.
- **No promotional or travel-guide tone.** Avoid "nestled", "breathtaking", "in the heart of", "vibrant", "rich", "diverse array of", "groundbreaking", "renowned", "commitment to", "natural beauty", "gateway to", "seamlessly". Keep neutral register even when the topic is nice.
- **No canned notability arguments.** Avoid "independent coverage", "featured in … and other prominent media outlets", "profiled in", "maintains an active social media presence", "covered by [type] outlets". Name the outlets once, factually, without summarizing their category.
- **No "refers to" lead sentences** — treat the subject as the thing, not the term ("X is…" not "X refers to…").

## 5. Formatting and structure

- **Headings in sentence case**, not title case: "Impact of technology" ✓, "Impact of Technology and Digitalization" ✗.
- **No bolded "key takeaways"**; don't bold every occurrence of a term. Bold only where the format requires it (e.g., first mention of a defined term).
- **No inline-header vertical lists** ("**Route Details:** …" bullets). Prefer prose; if a list genuinely fits, keep items short and unadorned.
- **No emoji as formatting** — no emoji before headings or bullets; no emoji in formal writing at all.
- **Em dashes: rare.** Humans use commas, colons, parentheses. Never use spaced em dashes ("word — word"). If you must use one, unspaced and sparing.
- **Quotation marks and apostrophes: straight**, never curly, and never a mix of both.
- **No thematic breaks (---) before headings**, no skipping heading levels, no tables where prose would do.
- **No Markdown-isms outside Markdown contexts** (no `**bold**` where the platform doesn't use Markdown, no ``` fences inside another document). In a Markdown-native context, use normal Markdown for real structure — the tell is formatting used as rhetoric, not formatting itself.

## 6. Chatty / assistant tells (emails, chats, first-person writing)

- No "I hope this helps!", "Of course!", "Certainly!", "You're absolutely right!", "Great question!", "I'd be happy to…", "Let me know if there's anything else", "Here is a detailed breakdown of…".
- No meta-commentary: "In this section, we will discuss…", "This article will explore…", "The purpose of this text is to…", "I have identified an area that requires updating…".
- No knowledge-cutoff disclaimers: "as of my last knowledge cutoff", "I don't have access to real-time information", "it's important to note", "it's crucial to remember", "worth noting", "may vary by jurisdiction".
- No placeholders: "[Describe the section…]", "[Your Name]", "2025-XX-XX", "INSERT_URL_HERE", "PASTE_LINK_HERE". Fill everything in.

## 7. What human writing looks like — emulate this

- Facts stated plainly, with simple is/has constructions and plain verbs.
- Repeated words; occasional mild hedges ("very", "perhaps", "probably", "tends to"); wordy human constructions kept ("as a result of", "in order to").
- Sentences that vary in length without a pattern; paragraphs that don't each end with a summary sentence.
- No summarizing moral at the end. It ends when the content is done.
- Specifics instead of categories ("the Mesabi Daily News ran three reviews" beats "regional press coverage").

## 8. Before delivering: run the self-check

Scan the draft and fix any hit:

1. Any word from the banned vocabulary list (especially 2+ in one text)?
2. Any "serves as / stands as / features / boasts / holds the distinction of"?
3. Any "not only… but also", "not just X, it's Y", "not X but Y" stacked?
4. Any emphatic three-item list?
5. Any sentence ending in a tacked-on ", -ing" flourish?
6. Any significance/legacy claims ("pivotal", "enduring", "testament", "broader", "legacy")?
7. Any "Despite its X, Y faces challenges" closing, or "Future Outlook" section?
8. Any emoji, spaced em dashes, title-case headings, bolded inline headers, curly quotes, or tables-where-prose-would-do?
9. Any "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "it's important to note", placeholders like "[…]" or "XX-XX"?
10. Any vague attribution ("experts say", "industry reports", "several sources")?
11. Any thesaurus-swapped repetition that should just repeat the word?
12. Does every sentence carry a specific fact, with no filler analysis?

If any check fails, fix it before delivering.
